SCHEDULING

Ask for a schedule change without hiding the impact

PhraseLane editorial draft · Updated 5 October 2026

AI-assisted draft. Human editorial review has not been completed. Review examples and translated output before using them in real work.

A practical multilingual draft for proposing a new time and acknowledging the effect on others.

Say what needs to change

A useful rescheduling message identifies the meeting or delivery, states the requested new time and gives the other person a way to decline. You do not need to disclose private details to explain a change. A brief truthful reason is enough when a reason is needed.

Offer a specific alternative

“Can we do it later?” sends the planning work to the reader. Offer one or two times with dates and time zones. Check both participants’ calendars, including daylight-saving changes, before writing. If a change affects a deliverable, say how and invite the recipient to flag a conflict.

Try this meeting request

Could we move our project check-in from 10:00 to 14:00 Japan time on 8 October 2026? I have a scheduling conflict. If that time does not work for you, I can also meet at 10:00 Japan time on 9 October. Thank you for considering the change.

Distinguish an invitation from confirmation

“Could we move…” requests agreement. “The meeting has moved…” announces an accepted change. Check that the translated message keeps the original intention. Leave the existing invitation in place until participants confirm, unless your established process gives you authority to change it directly.

Close the loop

Once everyone agrees, update the calendar invitation and send a brief confirmation using the same time zone. If someone cannot attend, agree on a separate update or handover. PhraseLane can help draft the words; it does not change your calendar or confirm the new time for you.
