CUSTOMER COMMUNICATION

A calmer workflow for multilingual support replies

Earlier beta article. This page describes the earlier twelve-language tool. The current English/Japanese workspace, usage limits and storage policy are described on the workspace, pricing and privacy pages.

PhraseLane editorial · 2 October 2026 · Practical guide

Create a useful first draft while keeping promises and customer data under control.

Separate the facts from the tone

List what you know about the customer’s question before writing a reply. Acknowledge the issue, explain the next action and give a realistic time for an update. Do not add a refund, discount or delivery commitment unless you are authorised to make it. Translation should preserve the promise you actually intend to make.

Remove information you do not need

A translation usually does not require an order number, address or payment detail. Replace unnecessary personal information with a neutral label before sending text to an external service. Keep the identifying details in your authorised support system and restore them only after the translated draft has been reviewed.

Use a small set of clear sentences

Try: “Thank you for contacting us. I understand that you could not open the file. Please tell us the device and application you used. We will review the issue and send an update tomorrow.” Adjust the example to match your actual support process. Do not promise a response time you cannot deliver.

Review the reply as a complete message

After translating, verify the greeting, the request and the next step. Check that a polite sentence has not become an absolute guarantee. If the issue involves safety, legal rights or a disputed charge, use a qualified reviewer and your approved process. Do not rely on a machine-generated response alone.

Measure usefulness with real feedback

Track whether customers understand the next step and whether your replies reduce repeat questions. Faster drafting is helpful only if it leads to clearer communication. Keep examples free of private customer information when discussing your process with colleagues or creating reusable templates.
