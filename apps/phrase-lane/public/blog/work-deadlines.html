WORK REQUESTS

Write a work deadline that survives translation

PhraseLane editorial draft · Updated 5 October 2026

AI-assisted draft. Human editorial review has not been completed. Review examples and translated output before using them in real work.

Replace “later today” with a date, time zone and clear deliverable in a multilingual work request.

Give the date and the time zone

“Today” depends on where the reader is. A colleague working across midnight may interpret it differently. State the calendar date, the deadline time and the time zone in the same sentence. If needed, also include an agreed UTC offset after checking the date’s actual time-zone rules.

For a simple example, write: “Please send the revised two-page proposal by 17:00 Japan time on 8 October 2026.” Avoid abbreviations that your recipient may not recognise. Japan time is UTC+09:00; other regions may change offsets seasonally.

Name the deliverable and the next step

A deadline without an object creates a second conversation. Specify whether you need a draft, an approved file or a short confirmation. If the task has dependencies, say what can be sent first: “If the final images are not ready, please send the text draft by that time and tell me when the images will follow.”

Separate a request from a promise

“Could you send it by…” asks the other person to confirm feasibility. “We will send it by…” commits your team. Check that a translated draft preserves that distinction. Do not let a polite phrase silently create a guarantee you cannot keep.

Try a short example

Could you send the revised two-page proposal by 17:00 Japan time on 8 October 2026? Please confirm whether that deadline works for you. If not, suggest a time you can meet.

Review before sending

Check that the translated date, time, document name and question remain intact. Keep the original next to the translation. Ask the recipient to confirm the deadline if it matters to delivery. For a contract or safety-critical handover, use an appropriately qualified reviewer.
