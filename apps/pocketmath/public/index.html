Know the numbers

Answers you can check yourself.

Where half a cent goes

A 50% tip on 0.29 is exactly 0.145. PocketMath rounds that tip up to 0.15, then adds it to the bill for a total of 0.44. The calculation uses integer units to avoid binary floating-point errors.

Read the money & rounding guide →

The cent left after a split

Dividing 10.00 by three does not produce three payable amounts that are exactly equal. One person pays 3.34 and two pay 3.33. The displayed groups always cover the original total, including extras.

Read the bill-splitting guide →

A discount on a discount

Take 20% off 100.00 and the price becomes 80.00. Take another 10% off that remaining amount and it becomes 72.00. The total reduction is 28%, because the second discount has a smaller starting price.

Choose the assumptions before paying.

These are arithmetic tools, not a record of what a merchant charges or what a group has agreed to pay. A tip is applied to the entire bill amount you enter. If you need a tip based on a different amount, calculate that tip separately and use the bill splitter to add the actual total and tip once.

The bill splitter assigns one additional cent to each of the first people needed to cover a remainder. It does not identify participants or decide who should pay more. Agree on that choice with your group. For unequal orders, use an itemized bill or a tool designed for individual balances.

All money inputs accept up to two decimal places and a maximum of 1,000,000,000.00 per field. Tip percentages accept 0–1,000%, discounts accept 0–100%, and percentages may have up to two decimal places. Group sizes range from 1 to 10,000. Currencies with a different smallest unit need a different rounding approach.

Calculations run locally, with no account, payment processing, currency conversion or stored calculator history. Review our privacy notice and terms, or send a reproducible calculation issue using invented example values.
